The challenge and opportunity

Value should not end with the cassava tuber.

Cassava processing generates peels that can accumulate around facilities in rural communities if they do not have a productive outlet.

01

Agricultural by-product

Cassava peels emerge from processing activity and can become a local waste-management concern.

02

Drying and milling

The peels are collected, prepared, dried and processed through hammer mills.

03

Useful ingredient

The resulting flour or grits can serve suitable fish, poultry and wider feed formulations.

04

Shared value

Agricultural material gains commercial purpose while operations create local participation and work opportunities.

Three dimensions of value

Practical impact across the value chain.

We describe impact carefully and do not publish unsupported environmental savings or employment figures.

Environmental

Productive resource use

Processing creates an alternative use for cassava peels that might otherwise accumulate around processing locations.

Economic

Commercial value from waste

Drying and milling turn an agricultural by-product into products that can participate in feed value chains.

Social

Opportunity in operating communities

Collection and processing activities can support gainful work and participation for young people in the communities where we operate.

How we approach sustainability

Patrick Stone Ventures creates productive uses for cassava peels generated during cassava processing. The peels are collected, dried and processed into flour and grits that can participate in suitable animal-feed value chains.

Cassava peels can accumulate around processing locations when they lack a productive outlet. Processing creates an alternative commercial use for the by-product while connecting cassava processors with feed-sector demand.

Yes. The cassava peel operation follows a circular-agriculture approach by turning an agricultural by-product into useful products for suitable applications instead of treating the peels solely as waste.

Cassava peel collection, preparation and processing can create opportunities for local participation in agricultural value chains. The model combines productive resource use with commercial activity around the communities where processing takes place.
